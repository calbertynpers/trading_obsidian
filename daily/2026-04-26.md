## Lessons / discipline

### Lesson 1 — Spot-Driven Long framework: first failure mode identified (ZBT)

The framework prediction: "if funding stays flat through a parabolic pump, the move is spot-driven and will resume after pullback."

ZBT had the textbook signature:
- +80% pump from $0.14 baseline to $0.252 high
- Funding stayed at +0.005% baseline through entire pump
- Daily RSI 86, ADX extreme
- Spot-perp parity (no premium)

Entry: LONG @ $0.2268 on what looked like the planned pullback to $0.215-0.220 zone.

**What actually happened:** Price continued to drop from $0.227 → $0.169 (-25% within hours of entry), bounced back to $0.193, and stopped out at $0.190 for ~−$202 loss.

**Failure mode diagnosis:**
1. **Spot demand can dry up faster than funding signals it.** We assumed flat funding meant spot bid was intact. In reality, spot buyers stopped without warning — funding takes hours to reflect that change because few perp positions had built up to need rebalancing.
2. **"Pullback to support level" ≠ "higher low confirmed."** Entry was based on price reaching the 1H EMA20 zone, not on a confirmed structural higher low. Should have waited for 4H to print HL above prior swing low, not just for price to touch a moving average.
3. **The framework's entry confirmation rules (5 conditions) were not all verified.** Specifically condition #1 ("4H higher low forming") was assumed from price level alone — should have required actual price action confirmation.

**Updated framework rules for Spot-Driven Long (added to [[Strategies/Spot-Driven Long]]):**
- Entry confirmation #1 must be a **printed** 4H higher low, not just a price tag of a support level
- Add new failure mode section: "spot demand can disappear without funding signal lag"
- Track this as the FIRST failed worked example to set the base rate

**Net economics:** Loss of ~$202 = manageable, position-sized correctly. The stop fired at the structural invalidation level as designed. Trade thesis was wrong; structure handled it.

### Lesson 2 — Stop hunting / cluster sniping is real and we're exposed to it

User raised the observation that TPs at obvious levels (round numbers, prior highs, BB upper) frequently get tagged just-shy-of and reverse, and stops at obvious levels get hit by wicks that immediately reverse. This is documented market microstructure (liquidity sweep / stop hunt).

**Diagnosis of our current orders:**
- ZBT TPs at $0.276 / $0.30 / $0.35 — first two are HIGH cluster magnets (today's high, round number)
- BSB future TPs at $0.65 / $0.55 — both round numbers, HIGH risk
- ZBT stop at $0.190 — round number, HIGH stop-hunt risk
- BSB stop at $1.045 — already off-round, OK

**New protocol drafted:** [[Strategies/Order Placement Anti-Cluster Discipline]]

Rule: TPs front-run the cluster (placed 0.3-1.0% inside the obvious level), stops sit outside the cluster (placed 1.0-2.5% beyond the obvious level). Round numbers and prior swing highs/lows are NEVER used as exact trigger prices.

Going forward, all new orders apply the fuzz convention. Existing orders will not be modified retroactively (avoid churn) but will be applied to all new placements starting tomorrow.

### Lesson 4 — Sustained Narrative Trend Long is a distinct category from Spot-Driven Long

AIN initially failed the [[Strategies/Spot-Driven Long]] checklist because:
- Funding was elevated (+0.04-0.06%, not flat baseline)
- Daily RSI was moderate (61.5, not extreme 75+)

But AIN was clearly a viable long candidate based on:
- Multi-week +105% real-product trend
- AI agents narrative stack (3 narratives intersecting)
- Strong multi-TF structure
- ADX 41 daily, 39 4H

**This is a different setup category** — should be formalized as "Sustained Narrative Trend Long" alongside Spot-Driven Long. Different time horizon (days-weeks vs hours-days), different signature (mildly positive funding sustained vs flat funding spike), different entry rules (split-tranche vs single conviction).

Worth promoting to formal strategy doc with AIN as the canonical worked example.
